# De-identification convention

How this family's information is written down. Read before typing a name, an address, a
number, a school or a provider into a conversation, a document or a file.

## The standing question

The team asks this in exactly two places, and both are hard gates rather than courtesy
prompts:

1. **Before the family profile onboarding starts.** The very first message of the onboarding
   is this question, before any question about a person.
2. **Before any personal or medical document is read, uploaded or summarised.** A school
   report, an IEP, EHCP, 504 or ILP, an NDIS plan, review or functional assessment, a
   paediatrician's or therapist's letter, a discharge summary, a photograph of a form.

> Before we go further: do you want this information de-identified? We recommend yes. If
> yes, I will follow the convention in this file, and the real details stay on paper in
> your house.

Rules around the answer:

- **Unanswered is not no.** If the family does not answer, proceed de-identified.
- Ask once per session, not once per document. Do not repeat it for the fifth upload.
- If a family has already declined to give a detail, do not ask for it again.
- If a family says no, respect it plainly, and say once that with a hosted model the
  conversation then leaves the house. Then continue without nagging.
- Record the answer in this file, so a later session does not have to ask again.

## The convention

The rows below are an example. Replace them with your own, or ask the team to build the
list with you.

| Instead of this | Write this |
|---|---|
| Amelia O'Shaughnessy, 17 | Miss A, 17 |
| 14 Wattle Grove, Reservoir | our place in Reservoir, near the park |
| 0412 345 678 | 0412 3 *** |
| amelia.osh@example.com | a@example.com |
| Reservoir High School | her school |
| Dr Nguyen, Reservoir Clinic | our GP |
| Born 14 March 2009 | 17 |
| NDIS 430 123 456 | on paper only, never typed |

The pattern behind the rows:

- **Names** become the form of address plus age. Miss A, Master J, Mum, Dad, Nan.
- **Street addresses** become the suburb plus one harmless landmark.
- **Phone numbers** keep the first few digits and mask the rest.
- **Email addresses** keep the first initial and mask the rest.
- **Schools, clinics and professionals** become "her school", "our GP", "our paediatrician".
- **Dates of birth** become the current age. The age does all the work.
- **Plan, reference and client numbers** are never typed at all. They are keys to real
  records and there is no task that needs them in a conversation.

## What to give generously

De-identification is not a gag. None of the following identifies anyone, and all of it is
the difference between generic advice and advice about this family:

ages; what the young person is like on a good day and on a bad one; what has already been
tried and failed; the sensory profile; who does the school run; the words the family
actually uses in the house for things; how much capacity the parents have this week.

## Where the real details live

On one sheet of paper, kept with the family's own records. That sheet is the only place the
two versions meet. It is never photographed into a conversation and never written into the
family folder.

## The honest limit

This lowers exposure. It does not make anyone anonymous. A suburb, an age, a school type
and one diagnosis can still point at a single young person in a small town. So give only
what the task in front of you needs, and prefer a name the family has chosen over one they
were given.

If a family realises mid-conversation that they have already typed something they would
rather not have, say so and offer to rewrite the affected notes without that detail.
